# LiftOn v1.0.14 — performance evaluation against v1.0.13

*2026-09-24. Focused campaign: 8 whole genomes and 34 subsets, one timing
replicate per arm. Evidence: `/ccb/salz3/kh.chao/lifton_improve/v14_perf/`.
Every table in the second half is printed by `report3.py` from `perf.json`
(`collect3.py`); nothing in it is typed in. Frozen candidate `3ab51cf`.*

## Summary

v1.0.14 is more accurate than v1.0.13 on every genome measured, recovers more
genes on distant pairs, emits GFF3 that validates where v1.0.13's does not, no
longer drops genes to per-locus errors, and is about as fast — 5 % faster by
geometric mean, with less memory on the two largest distant lifts.

- **Accuracy.** Mean protein identity rises on all 8 genomes and on 32 of 34
  subsets (the other 2 are flat to four decimals; none falls). On the transcripts both versions recover, 3,484 get better and 607
  worse across the 8 genomes; 9,624 and 164 across the subsets. The largest
  gains are on divergent pairs (subsets: human → mouse 0.779 → 0.822, rice →
  sorghum 0.677 → 0.720, C. elegans → C. briggsae 0.721 → 0.749).
- **Recall.** +1,068 transcripts on the 8 genomes against 15 given up, every
  one of the 15 traced to a cause and none a defect. 962 of the gains are the
  new second-locus placements; on human → zebrafish, 655 of 689 are supported
  by zebrafish's own GRCz11 annotation (null p = 0.001).
- **Validity.** `gff3-validate` finds 21 to 1,693 errors in each v1.0.13
  output — overlapping exons and overlapping CDS — and **0 in every v1.0.14
  output**.
- **Robustness.** v1.0.13 finished `partial_success` on 4 of 8 genomes. 422
  of its failures were spurious: features visited a second time as if they
  were loci, already emitted through their gene (dog → cat's 395 are all tRNA
  and rRNA rows present in both outputs). 4 were real: an empty-protein error
  cost a gene its lifted model and Step 8 stood in with miniprot models
  (chicken PPP6R2, ZIC4, STMN2; zebrafish RBFOX1). v1.0.14 emits the lifted
  genes (PPP6R2: 1 transcript → 59) and finishes `success`, 0 failures, on
  all 8.
- **Speed and memory.** 1.055× by geometric mean over the 8 genomes (range
  0.97–1.20×), excluding a database rebuild that lands on one arm by accident
  of order (see Caveats). Step 7 is 11–20 % faster on bee, gorilla, dog → cat
  and zebrafish. Peak RSS falls 1.4 GiB on human → zebrafish and 1.1 GiB on
  human → chicken; chicken is the one genome measurably slower (3 %; rice is
  flat), and why is known (below).
- **Determinism.** Both v1.0.13 runs of each genome are byte-identical;
  v1.0.14 `-t 1` equals `-t 8` byte-for-byte on rice, drosophila and dog → cat,
  and `-t 1` equals `-t 4` on all 34 subsets.

## How it was measured

- **Arms.** v1.0.13 (`b2fe59f`, the current release) and v1.0.14 (`ac7e863`),
  each imported from its own detached worktree and asserted on load. Shipped
  defaults, `-t 8`, identical argv. v1.0.12's release-validation output, made
  from the same aligner files, is rescored as a third LiftOn version.
- **Why `ac7e863` stands for `3ab51cf`.** The only shipped-code difference is
  the validator's phase check, which cannot change what a lift writes; a
  subset lifted with `3ab51cf` is byte-identical to the `ac7e863` one. The
  validity table uses the `3ab51cf` validator.
- **Same aligner input.** Every arm reads the same cached Liftoff and miniprot
  output (`-L`/`-M`). LiftOn never reads `-copies` itself, so the arms differ
  only by the LiftOn build, and the non-determinism of Liftoff's copy
  placement cannot enter.
- **One evaluator.** Every tool — the three LiftOn versions, Liftoff and
  miniprot — is scored by the v1.0.14 evaluator, which reads declared recoded
  stops through. Scoring the old versions with the old evaluator would have
  charged them for every selenocysteine UGA as a premature stop; this way the
  comparison measures the annotations, not the scorers.
- **Timing.** The two versions of a genome start at the same moment on one
  host (128 cores), two genomes at a time. Wall, CPU and peak RSS from
  `/usr/bin/time -v`; phases from `run_manifest.json` (wall clock).
- **Recall accounting.** Every transcript ID in each pair of outputs is
  compared, and each one v1.0.13 has and v1.0.14 lacks is classified by what
  now occupies its locus (`losses/`, `gainlose.py`).

## What the numbers mean

**Where the accuracy comes from.** Four v1.0.14 changes carry it, each
measured on its own before this campaign:

- `1963d5b` stopped reading miniprot's `stop_codon` row twice. That row had
  been replacing the terminal CDS of every miniprot model with a 3-bp
  fragment, so merges with miniprot failed or lost. With it fixed, merges
  succeed where they should. In D. melanogaster → D. pseudoobscura, CG8031
  goes 0.557 → 0.922 and CG7917 0.627 → 0.954, each now with the reference's
  exact CDS structure (CG8031: 6 segments of 49/82/69/113/112/463 bp, as in
  the reference).
- `transl_except` read-through: selenoproteins and declared read-through
  stops are no longer scored or cut as premature stops.
- declared genetic codes: mitochondrial genes placed from miniprot hits
  translate with their own code (dog → cat COX2 0.259 → 0.965).
- `9bdf8a1` keeps a 5′-partial CDS's initial phase through scoring.

**What was given up, and why.** 15 transcripts on the 8 genomes and 13 on the
34 subsets. None is a defect:

- *A completed neighbour* (most of them). When a merge that used to fail now
  succeeds, the gene's model spans its full intron structure. A gene nested in
  that intron — CG14683 and CG46459 sit inside pug's first intron in the
  D. melanogaster reference, too — is then suppressed by Step 8's span-overlap
  rule, which predates v1.0.14. Bisected: the fly losses appear at `1963d5b`.
  Suppressing by span rather than by exon is the lever; changing it is a
  recall feature with its own duplicate risk (Iterations 13 and 15), not a
  release fix.
- *Chicken, 12 rescue isoforms of genes that stay rescued.* 4 are SBF1
  isoforms now covered by PPP6R2, whose lifted model v1.0.13 lost to an
  empty-protein error; 1 sits behind a newly rescued selenoprotein; 3 scored
  0.390, just under the adaptive rescue floor, which v1.0.14's extra recovered
  genes nudged up (v1.0.13 emitted 7 rescues at 0.390, v1.0.14 4, and the
  counts at 0.391–0.393 are identical); 4 are isoforms whose widening would now
  reach a neighbour.
- *One C. elegans → C. briggsae transcript* whose two Liftoff CDS rows share a
  base. v1.0.14 refuses it by design (the shared base would be counted twice)
  and counts the refusal; v1.0.13 emitted it as an invalid model. A fix was
  tried and reverted (`1081a2f`): the full suite pins that refusal.

**ORF validity dips — because the models got more complete.** The share of
recovered transcripts with a start codon, a stop codon and no internal stop
falls slightly on 7 of 8 genomes (dog → cat 0.802 → 0.794). Of the 1,655
transcripts that lose it, 1,485 (90 %) move closer to the reference protein's
length, 1,456 (88 %) gain identity, and 238 gain an exact intron chain while 6
lose one (`orf_flips.jsonl`). v1.0.13's "valid" ORF was often a protein cut
short at a premature stop: dog XM_005624797.4 was 913 of 937 residues at
identity 0.973; v1.0.14's model is all 937 at 0.999, with the reference's exact
intron chain, and is counted invalid only because the cat genome really has an
in-frame stop there. The rest mostly start at miniprot's first aligned codon
rather than an ATG.

**Fewer `transl_except` values on some genomes, by design.** The dog
reference declares `aa:Other` on 344 transcripts — a premature stop in the
*dog* assembly that the protein reads through. v1.0.13 copied every value
verbatim, in dog coordinates, onto the cat models (246 transcripts). v1.0.14
writes a value only where the cat model has a stop at the aligned codon: 79
values written, 428 not applicable. Where rescued and merged models now carry
real declarations (selenoproteins), the count rises instead (human →
zebrafish 4 → 45, chicken 25 → 79).

**Warnings.** Warnings are advisory and dominated by one class of LiftOn's
own, `non_cds_phase` (a phase value on exon rows). It moves with the number of
models emitted: −10 % on rice and −6 % on bee, +0.5 to +3.4 % on the other six.
`cds_phase_consistency` rose far more in the first pass — 5,648 against 26 on
dog → cat — and that was the validator's fault, not the output's: it assumed
every CDS starts in phase 0, and v1.0.14 correctly keeps a 5′-partial model's
initial phase. Fixed in `3ab51cf` (N2 in the scan notes); re-validated, v1.0.14
has **0** phase-inconsistent transcripts on every genome, while v1.0.13's genuine
ones remain (26 on dog → cat, 4 on chicken, 2 on zebrafish, 1 each on rice and
drosophila). The tables below use the fixed validator.

**Speed.** v1.0.14 does more work — second-locus placements, more successful
merges, read-through scoring — and still runs slightly faster: Step 7 translates from a flat codon map and
clones attributes more cheaply (`c1657f9`, `c75f3a4`, `984834c`), and
reference feature selection issues one CDS query per locus (`3c9430b`;
`select_reference_features` 180 → 131 s on gorilla, 190 → 131 s on
zebrafish). The
second-locus pass costs 0.3–13.6 s per genome after this round's fix (M5; it
was 24–46 s). Chicken is 3 % slower: its rescue isoform scoring takes 55 s
longer for the same 46,241 jobs, the documented cost of `--rescue-max-inflight`
(v1.0.14 scores isoforms in batches of 8,192 with a fresh worker pool each, to
bound memory: chicken peak RSS 5.4 → 4.3 GiB, zebrafish 6.2 → 4.8 GiB;
`--rescue-max-inflight 0` restores one batch), and Step 7 has more to do —
PPP6R2's ~50 transcripts among them.

**The human same-species cell (MANE v1.5 → CHM13, the reporter's command).**
v1.0.13 → v1.0.14 changes 40 of 19,898 transcripts and adds 1: the 34 that
declare `transl_except` (selenoproteins 0.654 → 0.999), DDTL 0.193 → 0.296 and
TMEM255B 0.829 → 0.997 (merges that now succeed), TMEM52 (an overlapping exon
removed, same protein), SIRPB1 and STOX1 (only the CDS score column), and a
second TEX28 copy placed at identity 0.990.

## Caveats

- **One replicate.** Differences of a few percent in wall time are within what
  two runs of one build differ by on a shared host.
- **The aligner-input database step is excluded from the speed ratio.** Both
  arms of a pair read the same `-L`/`-M` files, and LiftOn caches their
  annotation databases next to them under an exact manifest that includes the
  LiftOn version. So whichever version did not write the cache last rebuilds
  it (gorilla and chicken: v1.0.14; zebrafish: v1.0.13), and raw wall clock
  charges that to one arm by accident of order. For a user it is a one-time
  rebuild after upgrading. Raw wall is in the table too.
- **Cached aligner input.** Liftoff and miniprot are not timed; v1.0.14 does
  not change how they are run. A fresh run's wall is theirs plus what is
  measured here.
- **Subset timings** ran concurrently with the scoring, at `nice 19`, on at
  most six cores; they show no difference beyond ±10 % (geometric mean 0.986×)
  and are not used for speed claims.
- **`LIFTON_RESCUE_ISOFORM_WORKERS=4`** was set in every arm to cap forked
  pools. Released v1.0.13 crashes with it set when a batch is empty (the defect
  `45c4ff2` fixes): 3 subsets (drosophila, cerevisiae → pombe, arabidopsis →
  lyrata) were re-run for v1.0.13 without it — byte-identical by the threading
  contract, but a different path for timing.
